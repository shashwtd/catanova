# Lobby settings and turn clock

New rooms start with a **90-second** turn timer. Before a game starts, the host can move it to any of the five slider stops, **40, 65, 90, 115, and 140 seconds**, or switch it off; switching it back on selects 90 seconds. Rooms created before this default keep the setting they were saved with. These are optional house rules layered over the unchanged base-game rules.

Only the host can change these settings. Every change is saved, broadcast to the room, and resets the other players' readiness. Ready messages sent against older settings are rejected, so a delayed message cannot consent to an unseen change. The host's **Start** button is their consent; the host does not need a separate Ready step. Every other player must be ready and every seat must be connected before starting. Settings lock when the game starts.

## What happens when time runs out

Setup settlements and roads are untimed. The first normal turn starts its countdown when setup finishes. Rolling, trading, buying, playing a development card, and reconnecting while someone else remains in the game do not restart that countdown.

At expiry, the server finishes the current turn using legal defaults:

- Roll the dice if the player has not rolled.
- Move a pending robber to a randomly chosen different tile, choosing a legal adjacent victim if needed.
- Complete any remaining free roads from a Road Building card the player already chose to play, using random legal sites.
- End the turn and withdraw any outstanding trade offer.

The timer never buys a piece, spends resources on an optional action, accepts a trade, plays an unchosen development card, or forfeits a player's seat. The ordinary rules still decide awards and victory during these actions. Random choices use the server's private randomness, and every accepted automatic action appears in move history with a timer explanation.

## A seven and shared discards

Rolling seven pauses the active player's clock while discards are outstanding. Each player who must discard gets their **own full configured duration** to choose cards. One player's submitted discard cannot shorten or restart another player's deadline.

On a discard deadline, the server randomly discards the required number of resource cards from that player's actual hand, without replacement. Card identities remain private; the public history records the discarded count. Once everyone has discarded, the active player's remaining time resumes. If that player's turn had already expired before the seven was rolled automatically, the server completes the robber move and ends the turn as soon as discards finish.

## Persistence and synchronization

The game server owns all deadlines. Room snapshots and ping replies include its current epoch timestamp; clients estimate the offset and render a countdown locally. Clients do not send timeout decisions or decide whether a turn has expired.

The deadline and paused/discard deadlines are stored in SQLite. Each game action saves the game state, clock update, event history, and command receipt in the same transaction. A storage failure cannot acknowledge a successful move or advance just the clock. Automatic actions use the same rule validation and commit path as manual moves. Their history entries carry `automatic: true`.

The server checks due clocks approximately every half second, in bounded batches, while at least one remaining player is connected. It also checks the deadline before accepting a manual action, closing the race between a click at expiry and the next scheduler tick. If that action was already saved, its receipt remains safe to replay; an unsaved stale action gets the latest room state.

A refresh or another device keeps the saved turn deadline while another player is present. When every remaining player disconnects, automatic progression pauses immediately; reconnect deadlines continue. If someone returns within their grace, the current turn and any required discard clocks restart with their full configured duration. The saved game retains its last committed choices, so a saved roll never needs rolling again. The server never fast-forwards turns to simulate elapsed downtime.

On server restart, previously connected seats get a new three-minute recovery grace from startup. Already disconnected seats keep their original deadlines, so restarting cannot extend them. Older saves using the former paused-grace behavior receive a one-time migration grace. This migration changes presence metadata only; even a long-running legacy game retains its turn count, board, resources and journal.

SQLite still needs a persistent volume and backups; this clock does not provide protection against losing the host's entire disk. Timer settings remain saved when a game pauses.

## Reconnect grace and resignation

In a started Classic game, a player who disconnects while someone else remains at the table is shown as away. After **30 seconds** a stand-in bot takes their seat and plays it until they reconnect ([how a stand-in plays](BOTS.md#covering-a-seat-somebody-left)). They keep their pieces, hand, cards and place in the order. Reconnecting hands the seat back in the same transaction that records their return; it does not reset their ongoing turn clock. Nobody is resigned for absence while any remaining player is connected. Intentional **Leave** immediately resigns the player and permanently releases their seat; it cannot be resumed. Closing a tab or losing connectivity is an absence, not a resignation. Lobby players never auto-resign. Big Table and Open Sea have no stand-ins; see [modes without bots](#modes-without-bots).

The **three-minute reconnect grace** applies once nobody remaining is at the table (see below); its countdown then appears beside each absent player's profile. A player whose grace runs out is resigned. A resigned player's roads, settlements and cities stay on the island and continue to occupy their sites. Their resource cards return to the bank; unused development cards are retired, not returned to the deck. Their buildings stop producing resources, they cannot trade or receive stolen cards, and they no longer qualify for either award. Future turns and setup slots skip them. With two or more remaining players the match continues; a sole connected survivor wins explicitly **by resignation**, without claiming a ten-point victory. An absent sole survivor has until their own deadline to return; absence never awards them a win. A player who returns after resignation may watch but cannot reclaim play in that match.

Required discards belonging to a resigned player disappear when their whole resource hand returns to the bank. Other players still finish their own discards. If the resigned player owed the robber move, the next remaining player moves it before rolling for their own fresh turn. Any unfinished free-road placements from the departed player are abandoned. Existing pieces are never removed to make these transitions possible.

If every remaining player disconnects, only automatic play pauses, stand-ins included. Every absent player's deadline becomes at least three minutes from the moment the table emptied, and then runs continuously. Once every remaining player has expired, the game closes as **Abandoned**, with no winner. It stays visible in history with an end date and cannot resume, but does not count toward wins or games played. A returning player never renews anyone else’s deadline. A connected resigned spectator cannot keep a game progressing while all remaining players are offline. Multiple departures that expire together commit in one transaction; their iteration order cannot select a winner. Leaving an already finished game preserves its original result.

## Modes without bots

Big Table, for five and six players, and Open Sea allow no bots: a host cannot seat one, and no stand-in covers an absent player. The rules in this section are Catanova decisions for the rulesets `big-table-v1` and `open-sea-v1`, whose rules are in the [Big Table rulebook](RULEBOOK-BIG-TABLE.md) and the [Open Sea rulebook](RULEBOOK-OPEN-SEA.md). Neither mode is offered to players yet. The absent-player rules below are built already (Release A) and apply to any ruleset without stand-ins, which a hidden test mode proves. Of the new clocks, Open Sea's gold-pick clock is built with Open Sea's engine; Big Table's come with that mode. Anything this section does not change works as described above for Classic.

### Absent players

A disconnected player is shown as disconnected, and the game waits for them. Their ordinary clocks keep running: the room's turn timer and discard clocks if the room has a timer, and the clocks of the Partner's phase, build windows and gold picks listed below, including the 45-second Partner clock that a room without a timer starts for an absent Partner.

Once a player has been offline for **2 minutes**, the turn clock acts for them at once whenever the game waits on them, exactly as if their time had run out. That covers their setup placements, their own turns, any discard they owe, the Partner's phase and between-turns build windows in Big Table, and gold picks in Open Sea. It uses the same defaults as an ordinary expiry, listed above and below; setup has its own default, described after the two lists below. It applies whether or not the room has a turn timer. The 2 minutes count from the player's latest disconnection, and the rule stops the moment they reconnect. After a server restart, a seat that was connected before the restart and has not reconnected counts as disconnected from startup for the 2-minute rule. A seat that was already disconnected keeps its original disconnection time, so a restart cannot postpone the rule for it. Like every expiry, it never buys a piece, spends resources on an optional action, trades, plays an unchosen development card or moves a ship, so an absent player's position stands still apart from the forced moves. Each forced move is logged as the player being away ("Ben is away; dice rolled automatically."), and the room wakes by itself when the 2 minutes are up, since no turn timer may be running to wake it. The seat's label counts down to the forced moves ("Auto moves in 1:32"), then says the clock is playing them, instead of promising a bot.

How this differs from Classic:

- Classic hands an empty seat to a stand-in bot after 30 seconds. The stand-in makes real choices for the seat (it builds, trades with the bank and plays cards), and the table sees a bot playing. These modes have no stand-in: the table sees the player as disconnected, the game waits for the first 2 minutes, and after that the seat makes only forced moves.
- Classic keeps a game without a turn timer moving through its stand-ins. These modes do it through the 2-minute rule, which is why the rule runs even when the room has no timer.

What is the same as Classic:

- Nobody is resigned for absence while any remaining player is connected, and **Leave** resigns at once.
- When every remaining player is offline, automatic play pauses, the 2-minute rule included. Each absent player's three-minute grace runs as described above, and once every remaining player's grace has run out the game is abandoned. When someone returns within their grace, the current clocks restart with their full durations, and the 2-minute rule applies again at once to any player who is still offline and has been for 2 minutes.
- Server restarts give the same recovery grace.
- A resigned player's turns and answers are skipped. In Big Table the Partner is counted only among players still in the game, and when fewer than five remain the Partner's phase stops and turns continue one player at a time. Between-turns build windows continue at any player count.

Setup placements are untimed, as in Classic, so a connected player is never hurried through the draft. A setup placement is compulsory, though, so it counts as a forced move under the 2-minute rule (Catanova decision). When the draft reaches a player who has been offline for 2 minutes, or a player passes 2 minutes offline while the draft waits on them, the clock places their settlement on the legal intersection with the most production pips, counting the pips of the number tokens on the hexes it touches, with ties broken at random by the server. In Open Sea only main-island intersections are legal. It then places a road on a random legal edge touching that settlement. In Open Sea, if no road is legal there, it places a ship on a random legal edge touching the settlement instead. If the player placed the settlement before going offline, the clock places only the road or ship. Without this default the draft would wait for an absent player indefinitely, because nobody is resigned while another player is connected.

### New clocks

| Window                     | Mode                           | Clock                                                                            | When it runs out           |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Partner's phase            | Big Table, paired turns        | Half the room's turn time, rounded up to a whole second, at least **30 seconds** | The phase ends             |
| Between-turns build window | Big Table, between-turns build | **20 seconds** per window, in every room                                         | That window ends           |
| Gold pick                  | Open Sea                       | **20 seconds** per player owed gold, for all their picks, in every room          | The clock chooses for them |

All of these deadlines are owned and saved by the server like the others above. Only the acting player's clock is shown prominently.

**Paired turns.** The Lead's part of the turn runs on the room's ordinary turn clock, with every Classic default: at expiry the clock rolls if needed, finishes a pending robber move and free roads, withdraws any trade offer, and ends the Lead's part. The Partner's phase then starts with its own clock. Half-second clocks round up to whole seconds (Catanova decision), so at the five slider stops that clock is 30, 33, 45, 58 and 70 seconds. A room without a turn timer starts no Partner clock while the Partner is connected. Its Partner clock is 45 seconds and starts only when the Partner is absent: when the phase begins with the Partner absent, or when the Partner disconnects during the phase. Once started, it keeps running to the end of the phase even if they reconnect. An absent player's own turn in such a room has no clock besides the 2-minute rule (Catanova decision; [Big Table rulebook, section 9.4](RULEBOOK-BIG-TABLE.md#94-disconnected-players)). When the Partner's clock runs out, the phase simply ends: nothing is bought, built, traded or played for them. Card effects left unfinished at that moment follow a Catanova decision ([Big Table rulebook, section 9.3](RULEBOOK-BIG-TABLE.md#93-when-a-clock-runs-out)): a robber move still owed by a Knight played in the phase is first finished with the Classic default, because a played Knight must move the robber, while free roads still owed by a Road Building card played in the phase are left unplaced rather than placed at random as at the end of a Classic turn. Discards after the Lead's roll work as in Classic: each player who must discard, the Partner included, gets their own full configured duration.

**Between-turns build.** After each turn the windows open one at a time, in seat order from the player to the left of the one who just finished. A player may close their window early. The game never skips a window on its own, even for a player who cannot afford anything, because that would reveal something about their hand. When a window's clock runs out it simply ends, and nothing is bought or built. The next turn starts after the last window. The window clock runs in every room, with or without a turn timer (Catanova decision; [Big Table rulebook, section 9.2](RULEBOOK-BIG-TABLE.md#92-clocks)).

**Gold picks.** After ordinary production, the players owed gold choose one at a time in turn order, starting with the player on turn. Each player owed gold has one 20-second clock for all their picks, which runs in every room, with or without a turn timer (Catanova decision; [Open Sea rulebook, section 9.5](RULEBOOK-OPEN-SEA.md#95-the-pick-clock)). If it runs out, the clock takes, for each card still owed, the resource the player then holds fewest of among those the bank can still pay, breaking ties in the order Timber, Clay, Sheep, Hay, Rock. The player on turn has their own clock paused while picks are made, as it is while others discard, and it resumes after the last pick. A second starting settlement's picks in setup have the same 20 seconds, although the draft itself is untimed (Catanova decision). The server keeps this in the saved turn clock: while picks are owed the player on turn's clock is paused and `goldDeadlines` holds the picker's deadline, and the next picker's 20 seconds start when the one before has picked. A room without a turn timer keeps a clock only while picks are owed, with no turn deadline, so nothing else about it is timed.

**Open Sea turn defaults.** When a turn runs out with a robber-or-pirate move still owed, the clock always moves the robber, never the pirate. If the pirate has already moved and only its victim is still to be chosen, the clock picks a random legal victim among the other players with a ship on its hex's edges. Each Road Building piece still owed becomes a road on a random legal site if possible, otherwise a ship on a random legal edge away from the pirate, otherwise nothing ([Open Sea rulebook, section 15.3](RULEBOOK-OPEN-SEA.md#153-clocks)).

## Validation

`tests/open-sea-room.test.ts` runs Open Sea's gold clock on a fake clock: 20 seconds with and without a turn timer, the paused turn clock resuming with the time the picks took, a turn that runs out rolling, waiting for the picks and then ending, an absent picker's picks made at once, and a whole game with an absent player. `tests/absence-rule.test.ts` runs the absence rule of modes without bots on a fake clock: no stand-in, the 2-minute wait, setup placements, turns and discards made for the absent player and nothing else, the pause of an empty table, restarts, and Classic's stand-in unchanged. `tests/turn-clock.test.ts` exercises accepted durations, host ownership, readiness resets, command reuse, exact revision conflicts, active and discarded-player deadlines, restart recovery, action/receipt rollback, automatic seven/robber/free-road transitions, manual deadline races, and live WebSocket broadcasts. `tests/disconnect-resignation.test.ts` adds real socket disconnects, reconnect cancellation, all-offline closure without autoplay, continuous deadlines across returns/restarts, one-time legacy migration, batch and manual resignation rollback/retry, permanent Leave, preserved pieces, conserved resources, and setup/discard/robber handling.
